Objects
Before importing: the object's front must face the +Y axis (Blender's green arrow). Use ↻ on a layer to turn an object that faces another way. Sizes are read in metres; check the size shown and fix it with the scale if the file's units were off.
Packages
A package mixes several objects in one placement. Import objects into its slots; each spot on the map gets a random one (the weight sets how often), never the same twice in a row. Each object takes its own width, and fronts line up.
Placement
Random transform min – max, per plant

Inner streets & islands
Click a space to make it a street (green), or press and drag across several; click again to take it back. The small cells where spaces cross are junctions. Streets reaching the edge continue straight on to the nearest street, up to the distance set in Join streets up to below. Areas between streets become islands, paved like the sidewalks.
Single objects
Click an object to pick it, Shift-click to pick more. Ids are row-column, counted from the top left.
Click an object to make it an empty spot (dashed outline); click a spot again to bring its object back. The other objects do not move. Press and drag across several to empty them all.
Objects alignment
Drag a rectangle to move it, its corners to add copies (it never shrinks below one object), the round handle to rotate. Blue shows the gaps; copies on the road are crossed out and left out of the 3D model.
Curves: select a placement and press Bend into a curve. Double-click the line (or press Add point) to add a point, drag points to bend the line, double-click a point to remove it. Copies fill the line and turn with it; drag an end point to make the line longer or shorter.
Automatic: Place automatically on islands (or Place automatically on a package) lays the object on the islands of the generated texture by rule: one in the middle of each side, its front (+Y) to the street, set back from the kerb; the weight is objects per side. A side too short gets fewer, a small island gets its longest sides first; nothing ever overlaps. It lays them again when its settings change or the texture is generated again; click one of its objects to select it.
Curve lines: Add curve line gives the rows more lines to bend them by (one per row at most): the first shapes the front row, the last the back row. A point added goes on every line. Drag a point along its line and only that point moves; drag it across and the points at the same place on the other lines move with it.
Foliage
Trees, bushes and other plants. A plant imported here is a layer, like an object: select it and add it to the map. Placements work as for objects (drag, corners, the round handle, Bend into a curve), and each plant can vary by a random scale, rotation and offset.
Plant packages
A package mixes several plants in one placement: import plants into its slots; each spot gets a random one (the weight sets how often), never the same twice in a row.
Decals
A picture laid on the streets: an arrow, a crossing, a manhole cover, a stain. A PNG with a transparent background; the top of the picture is its front, its width goes across the road. Each decal is a layer with where it goes. How they lie in the 3D model: 3D model, Decals.
Bridges
Place the red rectangle along the road that goes over. Drag it to move, a corner to resize, the round handle to rotate. Grey shows where that road ramps up and down; the road it crosses stays on the ground.
Settings
0 clean, 50 as learned, 100 dirty
Lanes
Every street's lanes are split evenly between its two sides, as many as its width holds (3 m a lane at least). Generate texture, then Set lanes and click a street to choose its own; Done lays just that street's lines on the texture (no new texture).
Markings area
Markings everywhere. Markings area puts a rectangle on the map: lane lines and decals only inside it (move it, size it from its corners, turn it from its handle).
Materials
Generate texture lays the street surface into the texture; its Top view and the 3D tab show streets, sidewalks and kerbs.
